Buy more properties later — from what the plan builds

A property is a later move, not a day-one one: pick a future age, and the down payment comes out of the resources the plan has actually built by then — HELOC room against the home, or a policy loan against the cash value. 0% business credit — the stacking accelerator

When it's on, the first five years of chunks land at the START of each year on 0% business credit (paid down through the year) instead of at the end — the same dollars hit whichever loan they're attacking a year earlier, and every later domino falls sooner. An accelerator on velocity banking, not its own engine.

Two separate dollars into the same premium: the tax reserve you're redirecting (sized by Section 1's tax bill), and anything extra you intentionally overfund to build cash value faster. The HELOC — the equity harvester

The rate and the home's own numbers. It funds future property down payments and, in retirement, covers whatever the properties and low-bracket qualified money don't. Real terms: a line has a ~10-year draw window from its first draw — inside it, interest can ride on the line (while room lasts); when the window closes with a balance, ALIS rolls it into a 30-yr fixed at your mortgage rate, whose real payment the plan then funds — and a fresh line can open against the remaining equity.

The policy — tax-free filler

Tax-free policy loans at the loan rate set in Section 2. The cash value keeps earning the crediting rate the entire time — even on the loaned amount (index loan) — so the longer it's left, the more it can lend. The policy is never allowed to lapse: new loans stop at 90% of cash value, and if drift ever pushes the loan toward the cash value, the plan pays loan interest in cash to hold it — a lapse would make every prior loan retroactively taxable, so the model treats it as forbidden, not just unlucky.

Other assumptions, in the open. Velocity chunking is auto-optimized across every open loan (home mortgage + any owned rental) — it always attacks whichever one carries the highest rate first, a guaranteed-return waterfall, then spills to the next-highest once the first is paid off; any chunk dollars left over after every loan is cleared now flow into the policy (or qualified room) instead of being lost, same as any other year with nothing left to chunk. Every property carries its own 30-yr fixed loan from its purchase date — the payment is a real bill in the plan, and unused paper losses (depreciation + interest beyond the rent) carry forward: they offset W-2 income up to the $25K allowance while working ($100–150K MAGI phase-out; unlimited with Way 1/2), offset other property income without limit, and shield retirement withdrawals up to the same allowance. Down payments for future purchases draw the HELOC or a policy loan at the purchase date — if the room isn't there, the purchase honestly fails. Closing costs aren't modeled; property loan interest uses a start-of-year approximation; the home value securing the HELOC is held flat until retirement. Depreciation runs ~27 years per property. RMDs start at 73 per the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table. Qualified caps at the employee 401(k) limit (no employer match modeled — if you have one, that's free money on top); a mortgage still alive in retirement costs its real P&I and reduces HELOC room (combined LTV). HELOC draw windows run 10 years; the refinance assumes a 30-yr fixed at your Section-1 mortgage rate. The policy cannot lapse in this model — draws cap at 90% of cash value and drift past 95% is serviced in cash (with a wash loan, the loaned slice really credits a lower fixed rate than shown — a licensed illustration is the authority). Unlock the write-off — three qualifying ways

The mechanism in three steps, then the three ways around the income cap. These are real qualifying facts about how you use the property — only turn one on if it's actually true for you.

Step 1

You buy a rental house. Not the one you live in — has to be a place you rent out.

→
Step 2

You pay for a report on the house. It unlocks a big write-off on this year's taxes.

→
Step 3

Check your income. That decides if you use the write-off now, or it waits.

If you make under $150,000

You use it right now — it lowers this year's paycheck tax bill.

If you make $150,000+

It waits. Nothing's lost — it banks up and pays out in full the year you sell the house.

Either way the write-off is real — it's only ever a question of now or later.

Way 3

Already own a rental making money? This write-off can cancel out that income — no matter what you make at your job.

Fixing-and-flipping houses also counts toward Way 2's hours — often faster than managing rentals. But the flip itself doesn't get this write-off: no depreciation, ordinary income plus a 15.3% extra tax, no 1031.
